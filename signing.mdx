[`vectors/signing.json`](https://github.com/paramxxyz/nautilus-paramx/blob/main/vectors/signing.json) has a worked example of each message: key, inputs, bytes and signature. The exchange's server and the Rust client are both tested against that file; test yours against it too.

## Keys and signatures

An account has two Ed25519 keys (see the [quickstart](/quickstart)):

| Key | Signs | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wallet key (the Sui address) | Sui transactions: trading key registration and deposits | Sui's scheme: intent-prefixed, Blake2b digest. Use a Sui SDK. |
| Trading key | Orders, cancels, withdrawals | **Raw Ed25519 over the message bytes.** No Sui intent prefix, no hashing. |

The signature is sent base64-encoded (standard alphabet, with padding): 64 bytes, so 88 characters. The trading key's public key is the raw 32 bytes, registered onchain with `new_trading_key_transfer`.

## Units

- **Micro units.** Prices and amounts onchain are integers with 6 implied decimals, like USDC: 1 USDC is `1000000`, and 2.5 shares is `2500000`. The `/v1` API takes the same values as decimal strings with at most 6 decimals (`"1"`, `"2.5"`).
- **Shares.** A share pays $1 if the underlying's price touches the tile's price range during its time window, and $0 otherwise. So share prices are between 0 and 1 USDC.
- **Prices are totals.** An order carries a total price (USDC) and a total amount (shares). Its unit price is `total_price / total_amount`.

## Contract ids

A tile is identified by a u256, sent as a decimal string:

| Bits | Field | Type | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 255–224 | `underlying_id` | u32 | the instrument id (`GET /v1/instruments`) |
| 223–192 | `start_active_ts` | u32 | window start, Unix seconds |
| 191–160 | `end_active_ts` | u32 | window end, Unix seconds |
| 159–96 | `max_strike` | u64 | top of the price range, micro USD |
| 95–32 | `min_strike` | u64 | bottom of the price range, micro USD |
| 31–0 | (zero) | | |

```text
id = underlying_id << 224 | start << 192 | end << 160 | max_strike << 96 | min_strike << 32
```

Example (in the vectors): BTC (instrument 1), from `1790000000` to `1790000300` (a 5-minute window), $109,000 to $109,100:

```text
min_strike = 109000000000, max_strike = 109100000000
id = 38195958776108886731205885586060617400434196320124015824815853142016
```

## Order intents

An order is the BCS encoding of one of the contract's Move structs. The contract checks its signature onchain, against the account's registered trading key, every time the order fills.

**BCS rules used here:**
- fields are laid out in order, with no padding and no length prefixes;
- an `address` is its 32 bytes;
- `u64` is 8 bytes little-endian, and `u256` is 32 bytes little-endian;
- `Option<u64>` is `00` for none, or `01` followed by the 8-byte value.

### Buy: `BuyerOrderIntent`

| # | Field | Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `user` | `address` | the account (wallet address) |
| 2 | `total_price` | `u64` | the most the order spends, micro USDC |
| 3 | `total_amount` | `Option<u64>` | the most shares it buys, micro; **none for a market buy** |
| 4 | `contract_id` | `u256` | the tile |
| 5 | `exchange_object_id` | `address` | the exchange (`GET /v1/exchange` → `exchange`) |
| 6 | `expiry` | `u64` | Unix ms; fills must happen before it |
| 7 | `nonce` | `u64` | random; see [nonces](#nonces-and-replay) |

It is 129 bytes, or 121 for a market buy.

### Sell: `SellerOrderIntent`

| # | Field | Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `user` | `address` | the account |
| 2 | `contract_id` | `u256` | the tile |
| 3 | `total_amount` | `u64` | shares to sell, micro |
| 4 | `total_price` | `Option<u64>` | the least it takes in, micro USDC; **none for a market sell** |
| 5 | `exchange_object_id` | `address` | the exchange |
| 6 | `expiry` | `u64` | Unix ms |
| 7 | `nonce` | `u64` | random |

It is 129 bytes, or 121 for a market sell. Selling more than you hold opens a short, which locks $1 of cash per share as collateral.

### Limit and market orders

- **Limit** (`"type": "limit"` in `POST /v1/orders`): both `total_price` and `total_amount` are set. Whatever doesn't fill right away rests on the book, at unit price `total_price / total_amount`.
- **Market:** the optional field is none. A market buy spends up to `total_price` at any price; a market sell sells `total_amount` at any price. Nothing rests, and the order is rejected if nothing fills.

### What the contract checks on each fill

- Both signatures verify against the two accounts' registered trading keys.
- Both intents name this exchange, and the fill happens before both expiries.
- The two orders are on the same `contract_id` and belong to different accounts.
- The prices cross: `buyer.total_price × seller.total_amount ≥ seller.total_price × buyer.total_amount`.
- Neither intent fills past what it signed. The contract records the amount filled per account and nonce.

**The fee** is 0.30% (`taker_fee_bps`), paid only by the taker, the order that fills against a resting one. A taking buyer pays it on top of the price: a buy's signed `total_price` bounds the price paid, and the fee comes on top. A taking seller pays it out of its proceeds. Makers pay nothing.

## Cancels: `CancelOrderIntent`

A cancel repeats the order's own fields:

| # | Field | Type | Value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `user` | `address` | the account |
| 2 | `order_max_amount` | `u64` | the order's `total_amount` |
| 3 | `order_nonce` | `u64` | the order's `nonce` |
| 4 | `exchange_object_id` | `address` | the order's exchange |
| 5 | `expiry` | `u64` | the order's `expiry` |

It is 88 bytes. The server checks that it matches the stored order, then takes the order off the book. Nothing is recorded onchain: the signed intent stays valid until its expiry, but only the operator can submit fills, and it only fills orders on the book (see the trust note under [bulk cancels](#bulk-cancels)). Only a limit order that is resting can be cancelled, and only before its expiry. `GET /v1/accounts/{address}/orders` returns each resting order's nonce and expiry, so a bot can cancel orders it placed before a restart.

## Bulk cancels

`POST /v1/orders/cancel-all` and `/cancel-batch` take many orders off the book with one signature, over the UTF-8 bytes of exactly this JSON (no spaces, keys in this order):

```json
{"scope":"cancel_all","address":"0x…","contract_id":null,"nonce":"42","expiry":1790000030000}
{"scope":"cancel_all","address":"0x…","contract_id":"3819…","nonce":"43","expiry":1790000030000}
{"scope":"cancel_batch","address":"0x…","intent_ids":["9c40d288-…","6d3a842a-…"],"nonce":"44","expiry":1790000030000}
```

- `contract_id` is a decimal string, or `null` for all contracts.
- `intent_ids` are the orders' intent ids, in the order sent. With `client_order_ids`, the server looks them up first, so still sign intent ids.
- `nonce` is a random u64, as a string, and can be used once.
- `expiry` is Unix ms, at most 60 s ahead.

The vectors include one of each.

<Note>
  **Trust:** a bulk cancel only takes orders off the book; it sends no chain transaction. An order cancelled this way still has a valid signed intent onchain until its expiry, but only the operator can submit fills, and it only fills from the book. The same holds for a single cancel. To limit that exposure, use short expiries.
</Note>

## Withdrawals

A withdrawal moves exchange cash to the account's own Sui address. The trading key signs the UTF-8 bytes of exactly this JSON, with no spaces and the keys in this order:

```json
{"scope":"withdraw","amount":"5000000","type":"0xdba34672e30cb065b1f93e3ab55318768fd6fef66c15942c9f7cb846e2f900e7::usdc::USDC","address":"0x…"}
```

- `amount` is micro USDC, as a string;
- `type` is the USDC coin type (`GET /v1/exchange` → `usdc_type`);
- `address` is the account.

<Warning>
  The message has no nonce or expiry yet, so a signed withdrawal could be replayed. The funds can only ever go to the account's own address; a nonce and an expiry are planned.
</Warning>

## Nonces and replay

- **Pick every nonce at random** from the full u64 range, and never reuse one. The contract keeps one table of nonces per account, shared by orders and trading-key registrations. A reused nonce continues the old intent's fill count, or is rejected.
- **A nonce's record can be deleted once its expiry has passed.** By then the intent itself is expired, so it can't fill again.
- **Expiry** is Unix milliseconds and must be in the future. There is no maximum today; an hour is typical.
- **Signatures bind the exchange object**, so an intent can't be used on another deployment.
